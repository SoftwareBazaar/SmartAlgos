# Quickmart IPO Kenya: Offer Price, Valuation Multiples and Investor Checklist (October 2026)

## The Short Answer

**Quick Mart PLC** is listing on the NSE via an **offer for sale**: Sokoni Retail Kenya Limited is selling **2.0 billion** existing shares (50% of the company) at **KES 7.50**, for a **KES 15.0 billion** offer and an implied equity value of **KES 30.0 billion**. The company itself raises **KES 0** — this is a sponsor exit, not a primary capital raise. At the offer price, management’s FY2026 adjusted figures imply roughly **13× forward P/E**, about **5.7× forward EV/EBITDA**, and a **~6.7% gross** dividend yield if the targeted payout is delivered. Independent analysts have published fair-value estimates **below** the KES 30 billion offer value, so treat the forward story as a projection, not a fact.

*Figures below are drawn from the Information Memorandum dated **2 October 2026**, the offer announcement, and publicly reported secondary commentary. Not investment advice.*

## Valuation Multiples at KES 7.50

The IM’s offer-price materials use management **adjusted** earnings. Using equity value $V = \text{KES } 30.0\text{B}$:

### Trailing vs forward P/E

$$\text{Trailing adj. P/E} = \frac{30{,}000}{1{,}705} \approx \mathbf{17.6x} \quad (\text{FY2025 adj. net income, KES millions})$$

$$\text{Forward adj. P/E} = \frac{30{,}000}{2{,}317} \approx \mathbf{12.9x} \quad (\text{FY2026 projected adj. net income})$$

Weight the **trailing** multiple more heavily: forward earnings are management projections, not audited results.

### EV / EBITDA (illustrative)

With net debt / lease-heavy liabilities of about **KES 7.27B** as disclosed around mid-2026 in the IM materials:

$$EV \approx 30.0\text{B} + 7.27\text{B} = 37.27\text{B}$$

$$\text{Forward EV / adj. EBITDA} \approx \frac{37.27\text{B}}{6.585\text{B}} \approx \mathbf{5.7x}$$

### Dividend yield (if the policy is delivered)

Management targets roughly **80%+** of annual profit after tax as dividends. A stylised **KES 2.0B** FY2026 distribution on 4.0B shares is **KES 0.50** DPS:

$$\text{Gross yield} = \frac{0.50}{7.50} = \mathbf{6.67\%}$$

Resident dividend WHT is generally **5%**, so net yield $\approx 6.67\% \times 0.95 = \mathbf{6.33\%}$ — **only if** the board declares that dividend. Policy is not a guarantee.

### Independent fair-value caution

Public commentary after the IM (e.g. Investia Africa, reported mid/late October 2026) has put fair value in a band roughly **KES 21–25 billion**, **below** the KES 30 billion offer valuation. That does not make the IPO “wrong”; it means the market may open with price discovery, not a free lunch.

| Store count | 45 | 64 (72 reported later in 2026) | Format mix: hyper / super / express |
| Net income | Much lower | Sharply higher | Profit CAGR far above revenue CAGR |

Kenya has a history of supermarket failures (Nakumatt, Tuskys, Uchumi). The IM argues Quickmart’s mitigants include professionalised PE governance, leased (asset-light) stores, and a working-capital model that keeps inventory days below supplier payment days. Those are **management claims in a selling document** — useful context, not proof that retail risk is gone.

## Worked Example: KES 10,000 Application

Rules: minimum **500** shares; thereafter multiples of **100**.

1. Shares you can buy: $\lfloor 10{,}000 / 7.50 \rfloor = 1{,}333$, rounded down to a **100**-share multiple → **1,300** shares.
2. Cash applied to shares: $1{,}300 \times 7.50 = \mathbf{KES\ 9{,}750}$.
3. Unapplied cash: **KES 250** (refunded if not allotted).

### Year-1 scenarios (illustrative only)

Assumptions are labelled; none are forecasts.

| Case | Allotment | Price move | DPS | Rough year-1 outcome on KES 10,000 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Soft | 100% (1,300 sh) | −2% | KES 0.30 | Small positive if dividend offsets price dip |
| Base | 100% | +8% | KES 0.50 | Mid-teens total return if both hit |
| Hot IPO | ~70% allotment | +18% | KES 0.60 | High headline %, but more cash sits in refund/MMF |

**Oversubscription** cuts shares allotted and leaves more cash idle. **No price stabilisation agent** is a material listing-volatility point in secondary sales of this type.

## Retail Investor Checklist

1. **Horizon** — Are you buying for multi-year dividends, or hoping for a listing pop? Secondary sales can be volatile on day one.
2. **Size** — Cap any single IPO at a small slice of liquid net worth (many planners use under **5–10%**).
3. **Channel** — Offer channels published for this IPO include USSD **`*483*803#`** (limits apply) and the web portal **`quickmartipo.e-offer.app`** with a CDS account.
4. **Failure mode** — Below **75%** subscription, expect a full refund **without interest**.
5. **Read the IM** — Risk factors, related-party history, lease liabilities, and dividend caveats live in the full memorandum, not in this summary.
